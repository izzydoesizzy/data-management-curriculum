The Caseworks track

Power BI for CaseWORKS agencies

Everything in this course was designed around one goal: taking data out of a case-management system like CaseWORKS (by VitalHub) and turning it into reports people actually use. This page connects the dots: what CaseWORKS records, what its own reports already cover, how to get the data out, and three extra projects built on Caseworks-style data.

Every agency's CaseWORKS is set up differently. It's highly configurable: programs, forms, user-defined fields and service codes vary from place to place. Treat the field names here as typical examples, and check them against your agency's setup with your CaseWORKS administrator.

2. CaseWORKS reports vs. Power BI: use each for what it's good at

Use CaseWORKS's own reporting for…

  • Funder and ministry submissions. CaseWORKS is one of the client management systems recognised for Ontario's Mental Health & Addictions Provincial Data Set (MHA-PDS), which funded agencies must start submitting by December 10, 2026. Never rebuild official submissions in Power BI; use the certified route.
  • Standard operational reports already built in, and dashboards for day-to-day workload monitoring.
  • Ad-hoc reports, which double as your export tool (see section 3).

Use Power BI (or Looker Studio) for…

  • Questions that cross modules: cost per client (services × billing), wait time vs. outcomes (referrals × assessments).
  • Combining CaseWORKS with other data: finance spreadsheets, funded targets, HR hours, census data.
  • Trends over time with your own layout, filters and drill-downs.
  • Custom views for different audiences: the board, a program manager, a team lead.

3. Getting data out of CaseWORKS

Three routes, from easiest to most powerful. Most people start with route 1.

  1. Ad-hoc report → export to Excel/CSV. Build an ad-hoc or custom report in CaseWORKS listing the raw rows (one row per service event, referral or appointment, not totals), and export it. Save each export into one folder with a consistent name, like service-events_2026-09.csv. Then use your Week 7–8 Power Query skills to clean, append and refresh. This is exactly what the course trained you for.
  2. Ask for a scheduled export or reporting access. Ask your CaseWORKS administrator whether exports can be scheduled automatically, and whether there is a read-only reporting database or data feed Power BI can connect to. What's possible depends on how your agency's CaseWORKS is hosted and licensed, so it's a question for your admin or VitalHub support, not something to work around.
  3. Watch for VitalHub's analytics platform. At its 2025 partner summit, VitalHub described a Unified Data Lakehouse designed for privacy-protected data, BI integration and secure APIs, aimed at making self-service reporting easier. Ask your admin whether your agency has (or will have) access.

Golden rule for exports: export raw rows with ID columns (client ID, staff ID, service type code). If a report gives you totals or merges cells, it's a report, not raw data, and you can't build on it. Ask for the row-level version.

Questions to ask your CaseWORKS administrator

  • Which ad-hoc or custom reports can I run, and can they export row-level data to CSV/Excel?
  • Is there a data dictionary listing the fields, service types and user-defined fields we use?
  • Do service types have costs or billing rates attached, and can I export that list?
  • Can exports be scheduled, or is there a reporting database / data feed for Power BI?
  • What de-identified fields am I allowed to export, and where may the files be stored?
  • Does our organization have Power BI licences, and who can publish and share reports?

4. Three Caseworks projects (after Week 14)

These use two extra practice files shaped like CaseWORKS waitlist and scheduling exports: referrals.csv and appointments.csv. Both are also included in the download bundle and in powerbi-starter.xlsx. Add them to your Power BI model the same way you did in Week 9.

Project A · Waitlist & referral flow 90 min

The question: "How long do people wait to get in, does it differ by program, and where do our referrals come from?"

Data: referrals.csv: ReferralID, ReferralDate, ClientID (blank if they never enrolled), Program, Source, Outcome (Enrolled / Declined / Withdrawn / Waitlisted).

  1. Load Referrals. Create a relationship Clients[ClientID] 1→* Referrals[ClientID].
  2. Add a calculated column in Referrals: 
    Days Waiting =
IF(
    Referrals[Outcome] = "Enrolled",
    DATEDIFF(Referrals[ReferralDate], RELATED(Clients[IntakeDate]), DAY)
)
  3. Measures: 
    Total Referrals = COUNTROWS(Referrals)
Avg Days Waiting = AVERAGE(Referrals[Days Waiting])
Conversion % = DIVIDE(CALCULATE([Total Referrals], Referrals[Outcome] = "Enrolled"), [Total Referrals])
On Waitlist = CALCULATE([Total Referrals], Referrals[Outcome] = "Waitlisted")
  4. Build a page: cards (Total Referrals, Conversion %, Avg Days Waiting, On Waitlist); a bar chart of Avg Days Waiting by Referrals[Program]; a bar chart of Total Referrals by Source; and a table of the current waitlist sorted oldest first.

Excel route: add Days Waiting with =IF([@Outcome]="Enrolled", XLOOKUP([@ClientID], Clients[ClientID], Clients[IntakeDate]) - [@ReferralDate], ""), then use a PivotTable.

Average days waiting (referral → intake) for enrolled referrals, 1 decimal?

Which program has the longest average wait?

What percentage of all referrals became enrolled clients (1 decimal)?

Which referral source sends the most referrals?

How many referrals are currently on the waitlist?

Project B · Attendance & no-shows 60 min

The question: "How many appointments are missed, and is it worse for some groups?" This kind of insight helps target reminder calls or transit support.

Data: appointments.csv: AppointmentID, ApptDate, ClientID, WorkerID, ApptType, Status (Attended / No-show / Cancelled).

  1. Load Appointments. Create relationships from Clients[ClientID] and Workers[WorkerID] to Appointments.
  2. Measures: 
    Appointments = COUNTROWS(Appointments)
No-show Rate = DIVIDE(CALCULATE([Appointments], Appointments[Status] = "No-show"), [Appointments])
  3. Bar chart: No-show Rate by Clients[AgeBand]. Line chart: No-show Rate by month. Matrix: Program × Status.

Overall no-show rate (% of all appointments, 1 decimal)?

Which age band has the highest no-show rate?

Project C · Caseload & workload balance 60 min

The question: "Are caseloads fair? Who is carrying the most clients and the most hours?"

  1. Active caseload uses each client's assigned worker, which is a second link between Clients and Workers. Rather than a second relationship, write a measure on the Workers table: 
    Active Caseload =
COUNTROWS(
    FILTER(Clients,
        Clients[AssignedWorker] = SELECTEDVALUE(Workers[WorkerID])
            && ISBLANK(Clients[DischargeDate]))
)
  2. Hours delivered (uses the existing Workers→Services relationship): 
    Hours Delivered = DIVIDE(SUM(Services[DurationMins]), 60)
  3. Table: Workers[WorkerID], Workers[Name], Workers[Role], Active Caseload, Hours Delivered, Total Spend. Add conditional-formatting data bars to the caseload column.

Excel route: a PivotTable on Clients filtered to blank DischargeDate, Rows = AssignedWorker, Values = Count of ClientID.

Which worker (WorkerID or name) has the largest active caseload?

How many active clients are in that caseload?

What is the highest number of service hours delivered by any one worker (1 decimal)?

6. Power BI or Looker Studio for a CaseWORKS agency?

Power BILooker Studio
Cost to learnFree (Desktop on Windows, or browser with a work email)Free with any Google account
Cost to share at workPro licence per user (often already in an agency's Microsoft 365 plan)Free to share
Relationships between many tablesStrong: full data model, DAX measuresBasic: "blends" of a few sources
Cleaning & monthly refreshPower Query built inLimited; usually needs Google Sheets first
Privacy controls for client dataMicrosoft 365 tenant, row-level security, sensitivity labelsGoogle account sharing; check your policy first
Fits most Ontario social-service agencies?Usually yes, since most run on Microsoft 365Good for quick, non-sensitive or aggregated dashboards

Recommendation: learn Power BI as the main tool. It handles CaseWORKS's many related tables, and it's what most agencies and job postings expect. Keep Looker Studio as a free option for simple, de-identified or aggregated dashboards. Either way, where client data may live is a privacy-policy decision (see Week 16), not a tool preference.
