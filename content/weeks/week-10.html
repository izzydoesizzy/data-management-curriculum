Why this matters: you said it yourself: "find relationships between data points." In a spreadsheet you connected tables one column at a time with XLOOKUP. In Power BI (relationships) or Looker Studio (a blend) you connect them once, and every chart understands the connection from then on.

This week's checklist

  • Understand the star shape
  • Connect the tables with exactly three relationships (or joins in a blend)
  • Prove the relationships work with a bar chart
  • Find the clients who have no services
  • Answer both checkpoints

1. Learn 20 min

Look at the diagram on the practice data page again. Our model is a star schema:

  • Services is the fact table in the middle. It records events and has lots of rows.
  • Clients, ServiceCodes and Workers are dimension tables around it. They describe the who and what, with one row each.
  • Each relationship is one-to-many: one client → many services; one code → many services; one worker → many services.

Filters flow from the "one" side to the "many" side. Click "Housing Support" (a client attribute) and Power BI follows the relationship to keep only those clients' services.

Read: Understand star schema (just the first few sections) and Create and manage relationships.

2. Do 35 min

  1. Open Model view (Path A: open the semantic model → Open data model, then the model/diagram view. Path B: the third icon on the left). Drag the boxes into a star: Services in the middle, others around it.
  2. Check existing lines. Power BI may have auto-created some relationships. Hover over or double-click each line. You want exactly these three:
    From (one side)To (many side)
    Clients[ClientID]Services[ClientID]
    ServiceCodes[ServiceCode]Services[ServiceCode]
    Workers[WorkerID]Services[WorkerID]
    Delete any other relationship (right-click the line → Delete).
  3. Create any that are missing. Drag the column from one table onto the matching column in the other (e.g. drag ClientID in Clients onto ClientID in Services). Check: cardinality Many to one (*:1) or One to many, cross-filter direction Single.
  4. Prove it works. Go to the report. Add a Clustered bar chart: Y-axis = Clients[Program], X-axis = Services[ServiceID] (set to Count). Each program shows a different number of services. Without the relationship, every bar would show the same total.
  5. Find the clients with no services. Add two cards: one with Clients[ClientID] set to Count (Distinct), one with Services[ClientID] set to Count (Distinct). The difference is clients on the books who have never received a service.
  6. Save.

3. Checkpoint 5 min

How many relationships does your finished model have?

How many clients have no services at all?

Notice what just happened: those clients exist in one table but not the other. A report built only from the services table would never show them. That's exactly the kind of gap that makes reports "terrible". Now you know how to find it.

Stuck? Try this
  • Every bar is the same length: the Clients→Services relationship is missing or inactive (shown as a dotted line). Double-click it and tick Make this relationship active.
  • Error about "many-to-many": you dragged columns the wrong way or onto the wrong column. Delete it and drag from the dimension table's ID column.
  • A line from Clients[AssignedWorker] to Workers? Delete it for now. Two routes between tables confuse filters. (Advanced models handle this; ours doesn't need it.)

Stretch (optional, 15 min)

Add a bar chart of service count by Workers[Role]. Which role delivers the most services? Then try ServiceCodes[Category]. Every dimension now slices the facts.
