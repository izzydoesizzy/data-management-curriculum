Why this matters: you've seen terrible reports. Usually the data was fine; the design was the problem: too much on one page, no clear question, pie charts nobody can read, numbers with no context. This week is the antidote, and it's mostly about decisions, not software.

This week's checklist

  • Learn the 8 design rules
  • Do the pie vs. bar experiment
  • Sketch my Week 14 dashboard on paper
  • Answer the checkpoint

1. Learn 25 min

8 rules for reports people actually use

  1. Start from the question, not the data. Write the 3–5 questions the page must answer before you add a single visual.
  2. One page, one purpose. "Where is our money going?" is a page. "Everything about clients" is not.
  3. Big numbers first. KPI cards across the top. People read top-left to bottom-right, the same as reading a page.
  4. 5–7 visuals maximum per page. If you need more, you need another page.
  5. Bar charts beat pie charts. People compare lengths far more accurately than angles. Always sort bars largest to smallest.
  6. Lines for time. Anything over months goes in a line chart, left to right.
  7. Titles that say something. "Spend by Program" is fine; "Housing Support drives 42% of spend" is better. Always show units ($, days, clients).
  8. Filters in one place. Slicers in a row along the top or a column on the left, never scattered.

Read: Tips for designing a great Power BI dashboard (Microsoft).

2. Do 30 min

  1. The pie vs. bar experiment. Add a new report page. Make a Pie chart with Legend = ServiceCodes[Category], Values = Total Spend. Without hovering, try to rank the six slices from biggest to smallest.
  2. Now copy the visual (⌘ + C / ⌘ + V, or Ctrl in the VM) and change the copy to a Clustered bar chart. Sort it by Total Spend, descending. Notice how much easier it is to read.
  3. Sketch Week 14 on paper. Read the Week 14 spec, then draw boxes on a sheet of paper for where each visual goes. Five minutes, no software. Professionals do this too.
  4. Delete the experiment page (or keep it as a reminder). Save.

3. Checkpoint 2 min

From your sorted bar chart: which category has the second-highest spend?

Self-check: rate an old report

Think of one report from your casework job. Which of the 8 rules did it break? Jot it down. That's your before picture, and you're about to build the after.

Stuck? Try this
  • Can't sort the bar chart: click the visual's … → Sort axis → Total Spend → Sort descending.
  • Pie chart has dozens of slices: you used ServiceCode or Description instead of Category in Legend.

Stretch (optional, 15 min)

Pick a theme: View → Themes (Desktop) or the theme/format options in the browser. Choose one with calm, accessible colours. Avoid using red and green as the only difference between things; about 1 in 12 men can't tell them apart.
