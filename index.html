A 16-week plan for Laura

Build the CaseWORKS reports you always wanted

CaseWORKS holds all the data, but its standard reports rarely answer the questions caseworkers and managers actually ask: How much have we spent per client? Which services cost the most? Who's waiting, and for how long? This course teaches you to pull that data out and build those reports yourself, in Excel & Power BI or free Google Sheets & Looker Studio, one short session a week.

Why this course exists

In casework, the reports coming out of the system weren't good enough, so colleagues exported the raw data into Power BI and built the reports they needed. The skill behind that isn't complicated. It's knowing how to take raw records, connect them, and add up, average, sort and group them. That's exactly what this course teaches, step by step, using data shaped like a real CaseWORKS export.

By the end of 16 weeks you'll be able to:

  • Read and tidy a raw export: spot duplicates, stray spaces and blanks, and fix them (Weeks 1–3)
  • Attach costs to services and calculate spend per client, per program and per worker (Weeks 4–6)
  • Automate the monthly report so next month's export takes one click, not an afternoon (Weeks 7–8)
  • Connect tables in Power BI or Looker Studio (clients, services, service types, staff) so every chart understands how they relate (Weeks 9–10)
  • Write the key formulas: total spend, clients served, average per client, length of stay (Weeks 11–12)
  • Design and build a dashboard your manager can filter by program, region or role (Weeks 13–14)
  • Answer real questions from a director, and ask for real CaseWORKS data safely and privately (Weeks 15–16)

Then the Caseworks track goes further: waitlist and wait times, no-show rates, caseload balance, a library of 12 report recipes, and a checklist of exactly what to ask your CaseWORKS administrator.

Who it's for: anyone who works with case-management data and wants better reports. No data or maths background needed. You need a Mac (or any computer), Excel, and 1–2 hours a week.

How this works

⏱ 1–2 hours a week

Each week has one main session (60–90 min) and an optional stretch task. Book it in your calendar like an appointment. Same day and time each week works best.

🗂 One dataset, start to finish

You'll work with a fictional agency's casework export: 300 clients, 13,000+ services and a cost per service code. It's shaped like a CaseWORKS export (clients, service events, service types with costs, staff), so everything transfers straight to real work data.

✅ Check your own work

Every week ends with checkpoints, exact questions with exact answers. Type your answer and the page tells you if you got it right. No guessing whether you "did it properly".

💸 Free tools, on a Mac

Everything works with free or already-owned software, on either path. See the free toolkit for setup details.

The big question we're building toward: a client has been with the agency for six months. They saw a community help worker 10 times, and each visit had a cost attached. How much did we spend on them, and how does that compare across programs, regions and workers? By Week 5 you'll answer it in a spreadsheet. By Week 14 you'll have a dashboard that answers it for every client with one click.

Your 16-week schedule

Click any week to open it. Tick "I finished Week N" at the bottom of each week to update your progress here.

Phase 1 · Spreadsheet foundations

Turn a messy system export into answers in Excel or Google Sheets: clean it, look things up, total it, pivot it.

  • W1 Set up and meet your data Get your free tools working, download the practice data and learn what a good raw table looks like. · 60 min
  • W2 Tables, sorting and filtering Turn plain cells into a proper table and answer your first questions with sort and filter. · 60 min
  • W3 Cleaning messy exports Fix the mess that real systems produce: stray spaces, inconsistent case, duplicates and blanks. · 75 min
  • W4 Lookups: attach the dollar amounts Use XLOOKUP to bring each service's cost into your services table. This is where the money appears. · 60 min
  • W5 Project 1: spend per client Answer the question that started all this: how much have we spent on each client? · 90 min
  • W6 Project 2: pivot tables Summarize 13,000 rows in seconds by program, category and client, with clickable filters. · 90 min

Phase 2 · Repeatable cleaning

Record your cleaning steps once (Power Query or a Sheets formula), then reuse them every month.

  • W7 A recipe for cleaning Redo Week 3's cleaning as recorded steps you never have to repeat by hand. · 75 min
  • W8 Merge, append and refresh Join tables in your recipe and add next month's data without starting over. · 90 min

Phase 3 · Dashboards

Connect tables, write measures, and build the report you always wanted, in Power BI or Looker Studio.

  • W9 Set up Power BI or Looker Studio Get your dashboard tool running, load the four tables and find your way around. · 75 min
  • W10 Relationships: connect the dots Link clients, services, codes and workers so your dashboard tool understands how they relate. · 60 min
  • W11 Measures: your first calculations Write the three measures every casework report needs: Total Spend, Clients Served and Avg Spend per Client. · 75 min
  • W12 Time: months, trends and length of stay Add a month column, see spending over time and calculate average length of stay. · 75 min
  • W13 Designing a report people actually use Learn the handful of design rules that separate useful reports from terrible ones. · 60 min
  • W14 Project 3: Casework Cost Dashboard Build a one-page dashboard to an exact spec, then use it to answer questions. · 2 × 60 min

Phase 4 · Capstone

Prove it: answer real questions with your own dashboard, then plan for real data.

  • W15 Capstone: answer the questions Five realistic questions from a manager. Answer each one with your dashboard. · 90 min
  • W16 Real data, safely, and what's next Plan how to get real exports at work, protect client privacy and keep growing. · 60 min

Ground rules that make this stick

  • Don't skip the checkpoints. They're where the learning actually happens.
  • Fall behind? Use the catch-up files. The practice data page has a clean version of the data, so you can jump into any week fresh.
  • Missed a week? Just continue. No need to restart. The schedule moves with you.
  • Stuck for more than 15 minutes? Open the "Stuck?" box on that week, then move on and come back. Getting stuck is normal; staying stuck isn't useful.
